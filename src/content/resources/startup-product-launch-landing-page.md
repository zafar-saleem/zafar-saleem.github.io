# How to Create a Landing Page for a Startup Launch

Launching a startup or introducing a new product requires more than building the product itself. You also need a way to explain your idea, reach potential customers and encourage people to take an interest.

A startup landing page gives you a focused place to communicate your offer and direct visitors towards a specific action.

That action could be joining a waiting list, registering for early access, booking a product demo or creating an account.

Here's how to plan a landing page for your next launch.

## 1. Explain what your startup does

Your landing page should make your product understandable to someone who has never heard of it.

Start with a clear headline that communicates:
- What the product is.
- Who it is designed for.
- What problem it addresses.

Avoid relying on internal product terminology or vague marketing language.

A first-time visitor should quickly understand why the product might be relevant to them.

## 2. Identify your target audience

A startup product may serve several types of customers, but your landing page should speak clearly to its primary audience.

For example, a project management product may target small business teams, while a financial software product may focus on independent professionals.

Understanding the intended customer helps shape the messaging, examples, visuals and calls to action.

## 3. Show how the product works

Product screenshots and interface previews can help visitors understand your offering.

Use visuals to demonstrate meaningful features and workflows.

If the product isn't ready yet, you can use carefully labelled concept visuals or explain how the product is expected to work without presenting unfinished features as available functionality.

## 4. Choose the right launch call to action

Your CTA should reflect the stage of your startup.

Before launch, you might use:
- Join the Waiting List.
- Get Early Access.
- Register Your Interest.
- Subscribe for Updates.

After launch, you might use:
- Start a Free Trial.
- Create an Account.
- Book a Demo.
- Get Started.

Use one primary action and make it easy to understand.

## 5. Build trust with useful information

People may be cautious about signing up for an unfamiliar product.

Help them understand your startup with:
- A clear product explanation.
- Founder or company information.
- Genuine testimonials, if available.
- Transparent pricing or launch information.
- Contact details.
- Relevant security or privacy information.

If you don't have customer testimonials yet, don't invent them. Focus on explaining the product and its value clearly.

## 6. Make the landing page responsive

Your launch page should work across desktop, tablet and mobile devices.

Pay particular attention to:
- Headline readability.
- Product screenshots.
- Sign-up forms.
- CTA buttons.
- Navigation.
- Page spacing.

Make sure the primary action remains easy to find on smaller screens.

## 7. Prepare for launch traffic

If you're promoting your startup through advertisements, social media, newsletters or partnerships, the landing page needs to be ready for incoming visitors.

Before launch:
- Test all links and forms.
- Check mobile layouts.
- Optimise images.
- Configure analytics where appropriate.
- Test sign-up confirmations.
- Review page metadata.
- Verify the domain and deployment.

A successful launch also requires a relevant audience and a compelling product offer. The landing page is one part of that process.

## How long does it take to develop a startup landing page?

The timeline depends on the page's complexity, availability of content and design readiness.

A focused landing page with an approved design can be simpler to develop than a page requiring extensive custom functionality, integrations or interactive demonstrations.

Agree on the project scope and launch date before development begins.
